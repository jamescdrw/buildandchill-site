One of the first questions people ask when planning an extension is whether they need planning permission. In Leicestershire, as in the rest of England, many single storey extensions can be built without a full planning application, but not all of them. This guide covers the basics so you know where you stand before you start.

Planning rules have conditions and exceptions, and they can change. Always check with your local council or the [Planning Portal](https://www.planningportal.co.uk/) before you commit to a design.

## Planning permission and building regulations are different things

This is the most common mix-up. Planning permission is about what gets built: its size, appearance and effect on neighbours and the area. Building regulations are about how it is built: foundations, structure, insulation, drainage, fire safety and ventilation.

You may not need planning permission, but a house extension will need building regulations approval. More on that below.

## Permitted development: extensions without a planning application

Houses in England have permitted development rights. These allow certain extensions to be built without applying for planning permission, as long as they meet a set of limits. For a single storey rear extension, the main ones are:

- **Depth.** Up to 3 metres beyond the original rear wall for an attached house, such as a semi or terrace, or up to 4 metres for a detached house.
- **Height.** No higher than 4 metres. If it is within 2 metres of a boundary, the eaves can be no higher than 3 metres.
- **Coverage.** Extensions and outbuildings together must not cover more than half the land around the original house.
- **Materials.** These should be similar in appearance to the existing house.
- **Position.** It must not extend beyond the front of the house facing a highway.

"Original house" means the house as it was first built, or as it stood on 1 July 1948 if it was built before then. Extensions added by previous owners count towards your allowance, which catches a lot of people out.

Single storey side extensions also have permitted development limits, including a maximum width of half the width of the original house.

If you want certainty, you can apply to your council for a Lawful Development Certificate. It is not compulsory, but it confirms the extension is lawful and is useful when you come to sell.

## The larger home extension scheme

For single storey rear extensions, there is a prior approval route that allows more depth: up to 6 metres for an attached house and up to 8 metres for a detached house, with the same 4 metre height limit.

It is not a full planning application, but you must notify the council before you start. The council consults your neighbours, and if anyone objects it considers the effect on their amenity, such as light and privacy. You cannot start work until prior approval is granted, or the council's decision period passes without a response. Your council can tell you how long that is and what to submit.

## When you will need full planning permission

Permitted development does not apply in every case. You are likely to need full planning permission if:

- **You live in a flat or maisonette.** These do not have householder permitted development rights.
- **Your home is a listed building.** You will also need listed building consent for most works, inside and out.
- **You are in a conservation area.** Rules are tighter. For example, side extensions are not permitted development, and the larger home extension scheme is not available.
- **An Article 4 direction applies.** Some areas or estates have had permitted development rights removed by the council.
- **Rights were removed by an earlier permission.** Some newer estates have conditions on the original planning permission that remove permitted development rights.
- **The extension goes beyond the limits**, such as being two storey near a boundary, or larger than the size limits above.

Your council can confirm whether any of these apply. In Leicestershire, that will be Leicester City Council, Charnwood Borough Council, Blaby District Council, Oadby and Wigston Borough Council, Harborough District Council, Melton Borough Council, Hinckley and Bosworth Borough Council or North West Leicestershire District Council, depending on where you live.

## Building regulations approval

Whatever the planning position, a house extension needs building regulations approval. Building control checks the plans and inspects the work at key stages, including:

- Foundations and drainage
- Structure, including any steel beams over openings
- Insulation and energy efficiency
- Fire safety and escape routes
- Ventilation

You can use your council's building control service or an approved private inspector. At the end you should receive a completion certificate. Keep it safe, as you will need it if you sell.

Some small porches and conservatories can be exempt, but a normal extension used as part of the house, such as a kitchen-diner or bedroom, is not.

## Other things to think about

### The Party Wall Act

If you are building on or close to a shared boundary, or digging foundations near a neighbour's, the Party Wall etc. Act 1996 may apply. You will need to give your neighbours formal notice before work starts.

### Drains

Building over or close to a public sewer usually needs agreement from the water company. In most of Leicestershire, that is Severn Trent.

### Neighbours

Even where you do not need permission, talking to your neighbours early can save a lot of trouble.
