A new kitchen is one of the biggest jobs most people do in their home. If you are thinking about a Howdens kitchen, it helps to know what a full supply and fit involves, from the first visit to the day you cook your first meal.

At Build & Chill Property Services, we supply and fit Howdens kitchens start to finish, with one team covering the building work, plumbing, electrics, plastering, tiling and decorating. Here is how the job usually runs.

## Why Howdens?

Howdens sells kitchens through trade professionals rather than directly to the public. Depots hold a lot of stock locally, which helps keep jobs on schedule, and there is a wide range of door styles, worktops, handles and appliances to choose from.

You can visit your local depot and work through the layout with a Howdens designer, then your fitter works from that design.

## Step 1: The survey

Everything starts with a proper look at the room. At the survey we:

- Measure the room accurately, including ceiling height, windows and doors
- Check where the water supply, waste and gas run
- Look at the electrics and the consumer unit
- Note the condition of walls, ceilings and floors
- Talk through what you want to change, such as moving the sink, adding an island or knocking through

This is where we spot things that could affect the design or the price, like a wall that is not straight or a waste pipe that limits where the sink can go.

## Step 2: Design at the depot

With measurements in hand, you can sit down with a designer at your local Howdens depot. You will choose:

- The layout and cabinet sizes
- Door style and colour
- Worktops, such as laminate, solid wood or stone-effect
- Sink and taps
- Appliances, integrated or freestanding
- Handles, lighting and storage extras

Take your time here: changes are easy on paper and hard once units are on the wall.

## Step 3: Ordering

Once the design is final, the kitchen is ordered. We plan the start date around delivery so nothing is stripped out until the new kitchen is ready to go in.

## Step 4: Strip out

The old kitchen comes out: cabinets, worktops, appliances and usually wall tiles. We take away the waste. Once it is gone, the true condition of the walls and floor is clear, and any small surprises can be dealt with now.

From this point you will be without a working kitchen, so it helps to set up a kettle, microwave and fridge somewhere else in the house.

## Step 5: First fix plumbing and electrics

This is the hidden work that makes the new layout possible:

- Water supply and waste pipes are moved or extended to the new sink, dishwasher and washing machine positions
- New sockets, switches, cooker circuits and lighting are wired in
- Cables for under-cabinet lighting or extractor hoods are run

Adding new circuits is notifiable under building regulations. Our electrical work is carried out and certified by NAPIT registered engineers. If you have a gas hob, the gas connection must by law be made by a Gas Safe registered engineer.

## Step 6: Plastering and making good

With the pipes and cables in, walls and ceilings are made good. That might be patching around new sockets, skimming a wall or replastering a whole room. Plaster needs time to dry before it is decorated.

If the floor is uneven, this is also when it gets levelled ready for new flooring.

## Step 7: Fitting the units

Now the kitchen starts to take shape:

- Base units are levelled and fixed
- Wall units go up, lined through with the base units
- Tall units, such as oven housings and fridge-freezer housings, are fitted
- Doors and drawers are hung and adjusted

## Step 8: Worktops, sink and appliances

Laminate and wood worktops are usually cut and fitted on site, with cut-outs for the sink and hob. Stone and quartz worktops are normally templated once the units are fitted, made off site, then installed, which can add some time to the job.

The sink, taps, hob, oven and other appliances then go in and are connected.

## Step 9: Tiling or splashbacks

Wall tiles or splashbacks go on behind the worktops and hob. Glass splashbacks are often measured after the worktops are fitted, so they may come a little later.

## Step 10: Second fix and decorating

The final stage pulls everything together:

- Sockets, switches and lights are fitted and tested
- Plumbing is connected and checked for leaks
- Plinths, cornice and end panels are fitted
- Walls and ceilings are decorated
- Flooring goes down if it is part of the job

Then we clean up and show you around your new kitchen.

## How long does a kitchen fit take?

It depends on the size of the kitchen and how much is changing. A like-for-like replacement in the same layout is the quickest. Moving the sink or cooker, adding new circuits, replastering, knocking through a wall or waiting for stone worktops all add time.

As a rough guide, a straightforward kitchen often takes a week or two of work on site, and larger jobs with layout changes or building work take longer. We give you a realistic timeline after the survey.
