Air conditioning is easy to forget about until it stops working properly. So how often should air conditioning be serviced? For most homes with a split system, once a year is a sensible routine. Units that work hard, such as in busy offices, shops, kitchens or server rooms, usually need servicing more often.

This guide explains what a service involves, what you can do yourself and the signs that your system needs attention.

## How often should air conditioning be serviced?

### Domestic split systems

For a typical wall-mounted unit in a bedroom, living room or home office, an annual service is a good rule of thumb. Many people book it in spring, before the warmer months, but if you use your unit for heating through the winter, any time of year works.

The manufacturer's guidance for your unit is worth checking too. Some warranties require regular servicing to stay valid.

### Commercial systems

Systems in commercial premises often run for long hours, and the air they handle can be dustier or greasier. These usually benefit from servicing every six months, or more often in demanding environments. The right interval depends on how hard the system works.

### Leak checks under F-Gas rules

Separate from routine servicing, the F-Gas regulations set legal leak-checking requirements for larger systems, based on the amount and type of refrigerant they contain. Most single domestic split units fall below the threshold, but larger commercial systems may need leak checks by law at set intervals. If you run a business with air conditioning, it is worth confirming where your system stands.

## What happens during an air con service

A service covers the parts you cannot easily reach or check yourself. A typical service includes:

- Cleaning or replacing the filters
- Cleaning the indoor unit's coil and fan
- Checking and clearing the condensate drain, and the pump if there is one
- Cleaning the outdoor unit's coil and checking it is clear of leaves and debris
- Checking electrical connections
- Checking the system is running at the right pressures and temperatures
- Looking for signs of refrigerant leaks
- Testing the controls and operating modes

Anything involving the refrigerant circuit must be done by an F-Gas certified engineer.

## Filters you can clean yourself

Between services, the simplest thing you can do is keep the filters clean. On most wall-mounted units:

1. Switch the unit off and isolate it.
2. Lift the front panel. It usually hinges upwards.
3. Slide out the mesh filters.
4. Vacuum off the dust, or rinse them in lukewarm water.
5. Let them dry fully, out of direct sunlight.
6. Slide them back in and close the panel.

How often depends on use, but every few weeks during heavy use is common. Your user manual will give guidance for your model.

Clean filters help the unit move air easily, which helps it run efficiently and keeps dust from building up on the coil.

You can also keep the area around the outdoor unit clear of leaves, plants and clutter so it can breathe.

What you should not do is open up the unit beyond the filters, use harsh chemicals on the coil or touch the refrigerant pipework. Leave that to an engineer.

## Signs your air conditioning needs attention

Book a service, or get an engineer to look at it, if you notice:

- **Weaker cooling or heating** than it used to give
- **Unusual smells**, such as a musty smell when it starts up
- **Water dripping** from the indoor unit, often a sign of a blocked condensate drain
- **Ice on the pipes or outdoor unit** when it is cooling
- **New noises**, such as rattling, buzzing or grinding
- **Error codes** or flashing lights on the indoor unit
- **The unit switching on and off** more often than normal
- **Higher electricity bills** with no other explanation

Some of these are minor, such as dirty filters. Others, like ice on the pipes, can point to a refrigerant issue that needs an F-Gas engineer.

## Why F-Gas certification matters

Air conditioning systems contain refrigerant gases that are harmful if released into the atmosphere. Under the F-Gas regulations, anyone who works on the refrigerant circuit must hold the right F-Gas qualification, and businesses that install or maintain these systems must hold company certification.

That means topping up refrigerant, repairing leaks or moving a unit is not a DIY or handyman job. Our electrical and air conditioning work is carried out and certified by NAPIT and REFCOM F-Gas registered engineers.

## Does servicing really make a difference?

Regular servicing keeps the system clean, catches small problems before they turn into costly repairs and helps the unit run as efficiently as it was designed to. A dirty, neglected unit has to work harder to do the same job, and that shows up in comfort and running costs.
