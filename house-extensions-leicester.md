## Planning permission and building regulations

Lots of single storey extensions in England can be built under permitted development rights, without a full planning application. Others need prior approval, and some need full planning permission. Flats, listed buildings and conservation areas have stricter rules.

Building regulations are a separate matter. A house extension needs building regulations approval whether or not it needs planning permission.

Our guide on [whether you need planning permission for an extension in Leicestershire](/blog/planning-permission-extension-leicestershire/) explains the basics. Your local council, such as Leicester City Council or Charnwood Borough Council, or the Planning Portal can confirm what applies to your home.

## How an extension project runs

### Design and approvals

We arrange the drawings and sort out the planning route and building control. This stage can take a while, so it pays to start early.

### Groundworks and shell

Foundations go in, then the walls, roof, windows and doors. Once it is weathertight, the inside work can begin.

### Breaking through and first fix

The opening into the existing house is usually made once the new structure is ready, which keeps disruption down. Pipes and cables go in next.

### Finishing

Insulation, plastering, flooring, tiling, second fix and decorating. Building control signs off the work at the end.

## What affects the price of an extension

Extensions are priced individually. The main factors are:

- Size and shape, and whether it is single or two storey
- Ground conditions and the type of foundations needed
- Roof style, such as flat, pitched or with roof lanterns
- Glazing, including bi-fold or sliding doors
- Steelwork for openings into the existing house
- Drainage, especially if there are drains under the footprint
- What goes inside, such as a kitchen, bathroom or underfloor heating
